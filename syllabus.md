# **Course Description**

Advances in biotechnologies, such as genomics, artificial intelligence and varied neurotechnologies, are expected to revolutionize human healthcare. Genomics, for example, has been applied to prenatal testing in order to screen for genetic disorders, to improve cancer treatment, and to diagnose rare disorders. Meanwhile, artificial intelligence has been applied to  diagnostic testing; it has also been used to develop  new treatments. And neurotechnologies have inspired hopes for creating new prosthetics and treatments for people with neurological or mental health disorders. 

At the same time, however, these and other advancements have raised troubling questions about global healthcare ethics, justice and fairness. Who, for example, benefits most from these advancements? Who is left out? Why, how, where, and when? 

This course tackles those, and other questions, head-on. It does so by considering real-world ethical dilemmas from across the globe. We will discuss how the medical knowledge that builds and is generated by these biotechnologies reflects and may contribute to health disparities between and within nations. Students will explore these biotechnologies in a global context, applying ethical frameworks to assess justice challenges, and examining models for their equitable and just development and use.

# **Learning Goals** 

**Global Perspectives Goals**

1. Develop the critical and ethical thinking skills and knowledge base for responsible local, national, and global citizenship.  
2. Develop in students an awareness of cultural diversity, as well as cultural self-awareness and the ability to recognize connections between cultural, economic, and social practices across countries.  
3. Provide students with a set of methods for conducting comparative or systemic studies of global phenomena. 

**Course Learning Goals**

1. Evaluate how existing social arrangements create and maintain justice issues, in different global contexts, in relation to genomics, medical AI, and neurotechnologies.  
2. Discuss biomedical research and technologies as part of knowledge systems that reflect and can contribute to societal injustices.  
3. Evaluate and apply different ethical frameworks for assessing justice issues in global health technologies.  
4. Compare different countries’ approaches to addressing ethical development of and justice in genomics, medical AI, and neurotechnologies.

**Lecture Participation Grade**

Your lecture participation grade is based on the below criteria:

**Attendance:** Attendance will be taken at each lecture. Students are expected to be prepared, on time and present for the full lecture. 

**Excused Absences:** An absence may be excused if students miss lecture due to illness, medical or personal emergency, or if students are representing the university in an official capacity (e.g. athletics, conferences). “Excused” means that students will have the opportunity to make up the participation points for this absence. Students can receive three excused absences.

**Unexcused Absences:** All other absences will be “unexcused.” For each unexcused lecture absence, students will receive a 1 point deduction from their final grade.

**Participation:** Students should be prepared and present for the full lecture. There will be opportunities during lecture for students to participate in activities and contribute questions and comments. Note that laptops and other screens are not allowed during lecture unless we are specifically conducting an activity using them. Students are asked to not wear headphones or earbuds. If you have an OAE that covers the use of laptops in lecture, please be sure that your teaching fellow has that information.

Communication is important – students should notify their teaching fellow regarding any absences or issues with attendance, in advance where possible.

As an ethics course, we will be discussing content that can be emotionally difficult. These  
topics can include, but are not limited to: race, class, and gender issues; health disparities; surveillance. Being able to learn about and discuss uncomfortable topics is an important part of learning in this course, but if engaging with specific content is so traumatic you cannot learn or be present, please let us know beforehand for accommodations. Likewise, if you become so distressed you need to leave a class, reach out to the teaching team afterwards for accommodations. We emphasize the importance of taking care of yourself and support you leaving a lesson that causes serious distress. Please reach out afterwards to let us know that you left class and so that we can check in on your well-being. In addition, we welcome feedback on how to better present this material or provide content warnings for students that may struggle in the future.
